{% comment %} Three stages, not four, and the copy is now true. "Locate" described distance markers narrowing a fault to the stretch of line it sat in. There are no distance markers any more - every household has its own hose from the source, so a leak names a household and there is no stretch to walk. "Detect" described flow measured against a calibrated pressure-flow curve; that calibration was removed as well, because it needed a field measurement nobody could repeat reliably. The page was advertising two things the system does not do. They are drawn along a line rather than as three cards in a row, because they happen in order, in one direction, along an actual hose. {% endcomment %}

What happens when a hose splits

  1. 1

    The line is read, every second

    A flow sensor and a pressure sensor sit on the hose where it leaves the source. The board beside them samples both every second and keeps the steady value, not the spikes.

  2. 2

    The pattern gives it away

    Water moving while the whole barangay is asleep, pressure falling with no tap open, or flow that simply never stops. Any of those for long enough and the board calls it.

  3. 3

    Somebody is told, by name

    The facilitator gets a text and the household gets one too, saying which line it is. Each hose serves one house, so the alert names that house rather than a stretch of ground to go and search.
